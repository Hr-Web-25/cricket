Heroes across generations

Legends who shaped Indian cricket

From the early days of Test cricket to World Cup triumphs and the modern era, these players helped change how India played, competed and inspired the next generation.

Legacy & influence

The players who changed the game

A selection of Indian legends whose performances, leadership and example left a lasting mark on Indian cricket. Several are members of the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame. citeturn1search2

The Little Master

Sunil Gavaskar

Opening batter · 1971–1987

Gavaskar became the first batter to reach 10,000 Test runs and showed that an Indian opener could consistently succeed against the world's best attacks.

Contribution: Raised the standards for Indian Test batting and became an early global symbol of Indian cricket.
World Cup captain

Kapil Dev

All-rounder & captain · 1978–1994

Kapil combined fast bowling, powerful batting and fearless leadership, captaining India to its first men's Cricket World Cup title in 1983.

Contribution: Proved India could produce a world-class fast-bowling all-rounder and inspired a new generation of cricketers.
Master Blaster

Sachin Tendulkar

Batter · 1989–2013

Tendulkar's extraordinary consistency and longevity made him one of the most influential sporting figures in India, with 100 international centuries.

Contribution: Inspired millions to take up cricket and set an unmatched benchmark for international batting excellence.
The Wall

Rahul Dravid

Batter & captain · 1996–2012

Dravid's technique, patience and adaptability made him one of India's greatest Test batters and a dependable performer in overseas conditions.

Contribution: Set an example of discipline and team-first cricket while helping India achieve landmark Test wins abroad.
Captain Courageous

Sourav Ganguly

Batter & captain · 1992–2008

Ganguly combined elegant batting with an assertive leadership style and helped build a confident Indian side capable of competing strongly overseas.

Contribution: Nurtured a generation of players and laid foundations for one of India's most successful eras.
Legendary leg-spinner

Anil Kumble

Bowler & captain · 1990–2008

Kumble became India's leading wicket-taker in both Tests and ODIs and remains one of only two bowlers to take all ten wickets in a Test innings.

Contribution: Made spin bowling a cornerstone of Indian cricket and set a standard for resilience and match-winning bowling.
Fearless opener

Virender Sehwag

Opening batter · 1999–2015

Sehwag transformed the opening role with an attacking approach, scoring India's first Test triple-century and later another 300-plus score.

Contribution: Changed expectations of how an Indian opener could attack from the first ball, especially in Test cricket.
Captain Cool

MS Dhoni

Wicketkeeper-batter & captain · 2004–2019

Dhoni led India to the inaugural men's T20 World Cup title in 2007 and the 2011 Cricket World Cup, while also taking India to No.1 in Test rankings.

Contribution: Established a successful era of calm leadership, finishing and wicketkeeping excellence across formats.
Women's cricket pioneer

Mithali Raj

Batter & captain · 2002–2022

Mithali became the face of Indian women's cricket for two decades, leading India in multiple World Cups and setting an enduring standard for women's batting.

Contribution: Helped bring sustained visibility and professionalism to women's cricket in India.
Fast-bowling pioneer

Jhulan Goswami

Fast bowler · 2002–2022

Goswami became one of the most successful fast bowlers in women's international cricket and a leading figure for India's women's team.

Contribution: Helped establish fast bowling as a major strength in Indian women's cricket and inspired young players.
Why they matter: These legends represent different eras and roles. Their achievements helped make Indian cricket more competitive, professional and aspirational for future generations.
